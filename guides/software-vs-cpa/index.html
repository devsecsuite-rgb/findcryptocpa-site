Crypto Tax Software vs. CPA: Which Do You Need?

Last Updated: November 16, 2026 | Reviewed by the FindCryptoCPA Editorial Team

The Short Answer: If you bought crypto on one exchange and held it, you only need software. If you used DeFi, bridged assets, traded NFTs, or received an IRS notice, you need a CPA. Crypto tax software costs $49–$199, while a CPA costs $500–$5,000+.

Every tax season, crypto investors face the same dilemma: Can I just use Koinly or CoinTracker, or do I actually need to hire a professional? Hiring a CPA is expensive, but making a mistake on your crypto taxes can trigger an IRS audit. Here is exactly how to decide.

When Crypto Tax Software is Enough

You do not need to spend thousands of dollars on a CPA if your activity is simple. Crypto tax software has become incredibly advanced and can handle basic scenarios perfectly.

Use software if you meet ALL of these criteria:

  • You only used one or two centralized exchanges (like Coinbase or Kraken).
  • You never swapped crypto-to-crypto (e.g., ETH to SOL).
  • You did not use DeFi (no staking, yield farming, or liquidity pools).
  • You did not trade NFTs.
  • You did not receive an IRS notice.

If that sounds like you, software will generate your IRS Form 8949 and Schedule D for a fraction of the cost.

When You Absolutely Need a CPA

A CPA is not just for filing taxes; they are for tax planning and audit defense. When your crypto activity crosses into complex territory, software will likely calculate your gains incorrectly, costing you more in overpaid taxes than the CPA fee.

Hire a CPA if you have any of these:

  • DeFi Activity: Yield farming, liquidity pools, and impermanent loss require specialized tracking. General software often gets this wrong.
  • NFTs: NFTs are taxed as collectibles (up to 28%), which is different from standard crypto.
  • Multiple Wallets: If you moved assets across 5+ wallets and bridges, reconstructing your cost basis requires blockchain forensics.
  • IRS Notices: If you received a CP2000 or Letter 6174, do not handle it yourself. You need an Audit Defense CPA.

The Hybrid Approach: Software + CPA Review

If you have a moderately complex portfolio but don't want to pay full CPA fees, you can use a hybrid approach.

Run your wallets through Koinly to generate the initial CSV/PDF reports. Then, hire a CPA for a 1-hour review. The CPA can look over the software's generated forms, check for obvious errors (like missing DeFi cost basis), and give you the green light to file. This usually costs $200-$400 total and gives you peace of mind.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a CPA use the reports generated by crypto tax software?

Yes. In fact, most crypto CPAs prefer it. Software like Koinly or CoinLedger can export a clean CSV or PDF report of your cost basis and transactions. You can hand this to your CPA, saving them hours of manual data entry and significantly reducing your CPA bill.

Is TurboTax enough for crypto taxes?

Standard TurboTax is generally only sufficient if you bought crypto with fiat on a single exchange and held it. If you swapped crypto-to-crypto, used DeFi, or have multiple wallets, TurboTax will not be able to calculate your cost basis correctly, and you should use specialized crypto software.

What is the cost difference between software and a CPA?

Crypto tax software typically costs between $49 and $199 depending on your transaction volume. A crypto-specialized CPA typically costs between $500 and $5,000+ depending on the complexity of your portfolio and whether you need audit defense.
