How Much Does a Crypto Tax CPA Cost in 2026?

Last Updated: November 15, 2026 | Written by FindCryptoCPA

If you traded cryptocurrency, staked tokens, or interacted with DeFi protocols this year, you likely have a complex tax situation. Many investors reach a point where DIY tax software is no longer enough and they need to hire a professional.

But how much does a crypto tax CPA actually cost? The short answer is: it depends on how complex your transactions are.

The Average Cost of a Crypto CPA

Generalist CPAs often charge $200 to $500 for a basic return. However, crypto-specific CPAs charge a premium because they must untangle complex blockchain data, cost basis issues, and IRS regulations. Here is the breakdown:

Complexity Level Typical Activity Average Cost
Simple Buying and holding on one or two centralized exchanges (Coinbase, Kraken). No trading. $300 - $800
Moderate Some trading, staking rewards, or using 3-5 different wallets. $800 - $2,000
Complex DeFi yield farming, NFTs, cross-chain bridging, or high-volume trading. $2,000 - $6,000+
Audit Defense Received IRS notices (CP2000, Letter 6174), back taxes, or active audits. $5,000 - $25,000+

What Factors Affect the Price?

When a CPA quotes you a price, they are looking at the amount of time it will take to reconstruct your tax history. The following factors will drive the price up:

  • Transaction Volume: If you made 50 trades, it's manageable. If you made 5,000 trades, the CPA has to use specialized software to reconcile everything.
  • DeFi and NFTs: DeFi creates a massive amount of micro-transactions (gas fees, yield harvesting, liquidity pool deposits). NFTs are subject to the 28% collectible tax rate. Both require extra work.
  • Missing Records: If you lost access to an old wallet or an exchange shut down, the CPA has to use blockchain forensics to reconstruct your cost basis.
  • Amended Returns: If you need to file a Form 1040-X to correct previous years, expect to pay double the standard fee.

Why a Cheap CPA is Actually Expensive

Many investors try to save money by hiring a local CPA who charges $250. However, if that CPA has never dealt with DeFi or cross-chain bridging, they will likely calculate your cost basis incorrectly.

This leads to two outcomes:

  • You overpay taxes: They might not know how to account for impermanent loss or gas fees, causing you to miss out on deductions.
  • You trigger an audit: If they report your crypto gains incorrectly, the IRS will cross-reference the 1099-K forms from your exchanges and flag you. A crypto specialist knows exactly how to report to avoid red flags.

Official IRS Resources: Always verify tax information with the IRS Virtual Currency Guidance (.gov).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a crypto tax CPA tax-deductible?

Yes, if you are running a business or trading as a business, the fee you pay a CPA is tax-deductible as a business expense. For personal tax returns, the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act suspended miscellaneous itemized deductions, so you generally cannot deduct personal tax prep fees.

Can I just use TurboTax for crypto?

TurboTax and standard tax software are only equipped to handle very simple crypto situations (like buying and holding Bitcoin on one exchange). If you have swapped crypto-to-crypto, used DeFi, or have multiple wallets, you need specialized crypto software like Koinly or a human CPA.

Why is a cheap CPA actually expensive?

A generalist CPA who charges $200 but does not understand DeFi cost basis or cross-chain bridging will likely make mistakes. These mistakes can lead to overpaying taxes, or worse, an IRS audit and penalties. Specialized knowledge is always worth the premium.
