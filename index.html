Quick Checklist: Before You Hire a Crypto CPA

  • Export all transaction CSVs from every exchange
  • List every wallet address you own or control
  • Document any DeFi activity — LPs, staking, lending, borrowing
  • Note any NFT purchases, sales, or mints
  • Download any 1099 forms your exchanges issued
  • Prepare your cost basis method preference — FIFO, LIFO, HIFO, or Specific ID
  • Gather records of any prior IRS correspondence

How to Choose a Crypto Tax CPA

  • Check credentials: CPA, EA, or JD with crypto tax specialization
  • Verify software experience: Koinly, CoinTracker, TokenTax, CoinLedger
  • Ask about audit defense: Can they represent you before the IRS?
  • Confirm DeFi/NFT expertise: Do they understand your specific activity?
  • Request pricing upfront: Flat fee vs hourly, and what's included
  • Check references: Ask for 2–3 client testimonials

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need a crypto tax CPA?

Not always. If you only bought and held crypto on a single exchange, crypto tax software like Koinly or CoinTracker can handle it for $49-$199. You need a CPA if you used DeFi, traded NFTs, bridged across chains, received airdrops, mined, or received any IRS notice. Take our complexity quiz to find out where you fall.

How much does a crypto tax CPA cost?

Typical range is $300 to $1,500 for individual returns, depending on complexity. Complex DeFi situations with hundreds of transactions can cost $1,000-$3,000. Hourly rates range from $150 to $500. Our directory lists price ranges for every CPA so you can filter by budget.

What should I have ready before contacting a CPA?

Gather: (1) CSV exports from every exchange you used, (2) wallet addresses for on-chain activity, (3) your crypto tax software report (if you have one), (4) any 1099 forms from exchanges, (5) records of any IRS notices. The more organized you are, the less the CPA charges.

Can a regular CPA handle my crypto taxes?

Often no. Crypto tax requires understanding of cost basis methods (FIFO, LIFO, HIFO, specific ID), DeFi income recognition, NFT collectible rules at 28%, and cross-chain reporting. A general CPA may file your return but miss deductions or trigger audits. Always ask if they handle crypto specifically before hiring.
