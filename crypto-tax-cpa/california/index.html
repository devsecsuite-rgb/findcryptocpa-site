Crypto Tax Rules in California

California has no preferential capital gains rate. Every crypto gain—whether held for five days or five years—is taxed as ordinary income at progressive rates from 1% to 13.3%. The top 13.3% bracket includes a 1% Mental Health Services Tax on income over $1 million. California also does not fully conform to federal like-kind exchange rules, and as a community property state, crypto acquired during marriage is divided 50/50 in a divorce.

California Audit Triggers & Enforcement

The FTB aggressively audits residency claims for high earners who leave California with unrealized crypto positions, scrutinizing the exact date of sale relative to the move. They also cross-reference 1099-B and 1099-DA data from exchanges to catch unreported crypto-to-crypto swaps.

Frequently Asked Questions: California Crypto Taxes

Why does California tax long-term crypto gains at the same rate as short-term gains?

The California Franchise Tax Board does not distinguish between holding periods. All capital gains, including crypto, are added to your ordinary income and taxed at progressive rates up to 13.3%. Federally, a long-term gain may qualify for a 20% rate, but California still taxes that same gain as if it were salary, eliminating the state-level benefit of holding longer than one year.

How does the FTB audit cryptocurrency transactions?

The FTB receives 1099-B and 1099-DA data from major exchanges. The FTB also conducts aggressive residency audits for high earners who claim to have moved to no-income-tax states like Florida or Texas, scrutinizing the timing of crypto sales relative to the move.

How is cryptocurrency divided in a California divorce?

Under California Family Code § 760, crypto acquired during the marriage is community property and is generally divided 50/50. Transfers between spouses incident to divorce are typically tax-free under IRC Section 1041, but if crypto must be sold to fund a buyout, that sale triggers capital gains tax at California's ordinary income rates.

What is California's 2026 crypto transaction surcharge?

Recent legislation introduced a 0.75% 'crypto transaction surcharge' on the sale of digital assets over $100,000 to fund consumer education. This is applied on top of standard capital gains taxes.
