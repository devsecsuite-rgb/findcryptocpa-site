Crypto Tax Rules in New York

New York taxes crypto gains as capital gains, with top state rates reaching 10.9%. If you live in New York City, you may also be subject to local city income tax, pushing the combined top rate even higher. New York also requires crypto businesses operating in the state to hold a BitLicense, which carries specific tax and reporting implications for business owners.

New York Audit Triggers & Enforcement

New York aggressively pursues 'convenience of the employer' rules, meaning remote workers who claim residency outside NY but work for a NY-based crypto company may still owe NY state tax. The state also audits high-volume traders for failure to pay NYC estimated taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions: New York Crypto Taxes

How much is the combined New York State and NYC crypto tax?

New York State taxes crypto gains as ordinary income up to 10.9%. If you live in New York City, you pay an additional 3.078% to 3.876% local income tax, pushing the combined top burden to 14.776%.

Does the BitLicense affect individual crypto investors?

No. The BitLicense applies to crypto businesses operating in New York, not to individual investors. However, it means New York has some of the most rigorous compliance and reporting standards for crypto platforms.

What is the 'convenience of the employer' rule?

If you live outside New York but work remotely for a New York-based crypto company, New York may still tax your income (including crypto bonuses) under this rule, unless your work is absolutely necessary outside the state.

I live in Yonkers. Is there an additional local crypto tax?

Yes. Yonkers residents are subject to a 16.75% surtax on their New York State income tax liability. This applies to crypto capital gains and ordinary income earned while residing in Yonkers.
