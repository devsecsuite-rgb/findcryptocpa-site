Facing a Crypto Tax Audit or IRS Notice?

The IRS has dramatically increased enforcement on cryptocurrency. In 2025 alone, the agency sent hundreds of thousands of automated warning letters and launched a 52% increase in crypto-related audits compared to the previous year. If you have traded, staked, mined, or sold crypto and did not report it perfectly, you are at risk.

If you received an IRS notice in the mail

Do not panic, and do not respond directly to the IRS. Anything you say can be used against you. It is highly recommended to let a licensed CPA handle the correspondence via Form 2848 (Power of Attorney).

If you recently received a letter from the IRS regarding your digital assets, read our comprehensive guide on how to respond to IRS Letter 6174 and CP2000 notices immediately.

What Does a Crypto CPA Do in an Audit?

An experienced crypto CPA does far more than just file your taxes. When you hire an audit defense specialist, they act as your shield. Here is exactly how they protect you:

1. IRS Representation (Form 2848)

The CPA will file Form 2848, which grants them Power of Attorney. This means the IRS can no longer contact you directly; they must speak only to your CPA. This removes the stress and prevents you from accidentally incriminating yourself during an interview.

2. Penalty Abatement

If the IRS is assessing fines for late payment or underreporting, a CPA can request First-Time Penalty Abatement. If you have a clean 3-year compliance history, they can often get the penalties completely wiped out. They may also argue "reasonable cause" if you relied on faulty tax software or bad advice.

3. Cost Basis Reconstruction

Most crypto audits happen because the IRS received a 1099-K or 1099-MISC from an exchange like Coinbase, but your tax return did not match that number. A CPA will use blockchain forensics and exchange records to reconstruct your cost basis and prove that you do not owe the taxes the IRS claims you do.

Types of IRS Crypto Notices

The IRS uses different letters depending on how severe they believe your non-compliance is.

  • Letter 6174 / 6174-A: An automated "educational" notice. The IRS simply sees crypto activity and wants to remind you to report it. This is a soft warning.
  • CP2000: A formal "Proposed Adjustment" notice. The IRS has calculated what they believe you owe, including taxes and penalties. You have 30 days to respond. This is very serious.
  • CP2501: A notice stating the IRS has information that doesn't match your return. They are asking for an explanation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just ignore the IRS letter if I think it's a mistake?

No. Ignoring an IRS notice—even an educational one like 6174—will escalate the situation. The IRS will assume you are non-compliant and may issue a formal CP2000 audit notice, which carries higher penalties.

I use a hardware wallet. Why did the IRS contact me?

Exchanges (like Coinbase or Kraken) report your activity to the IRS when you cash out or when you meet certain thresholds. Even if you moved funds to a hardware wallet, the exchange reports the initial movement. If you didn't report it as a transfer (and instead the IRS thinks you sold it), their system flags you.

How much does a crypto audit defense CPA cost?

Representation costs vary. Simple notice responses might cost $1,000–$3,000. A full audit defense with back taxes and penalty abatement can range from $5,000 to $25,000 or more, depending on the complexity of your trades and the number of years involved.
