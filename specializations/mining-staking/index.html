Why mining and staking tax is different

Mining and staking rewards are treated as ordinary income at the fair market value when received. When you sell the coins later, you recognize a capital gain or loss on the difference between the income value and the sale price — a double taxation structure many miners miss.

What you'll owe tax on

  • Income recognition: FMV of rewards at receipt = ordinary income
  • Staking: IRS treats rewards as income at receipt (Rev. Rul. 2023-14)
  • Node operations: Validator rewards, slashing losses, and hosting costs all factor into basis

What's deductible

  • Equipment depreciation: Mining rigs, ASICs, and GPUs can be depreciated under Section 179 or MACRS
  • Electricity: Deductible as a business expense for hobby vs. business miners

Mining & Staking Tax: Key Statistics

  • US crypto miners' share of global hashrate: 38%
  • Staking rewards are taxed as ordinary income at rates up to 37%
  • Average staking tax liability for $50K in rewards: $12,000–$18,500
  • Self-employment tax adds 15.3% on top of income tax for miners
